03_ The wheel

Eight sectors. Everything inside reach.

Hover a sector to arm it. Each one opens a small panel of the handful of actions that belong to it.

AEwheel · in the comp

Drawn from the plugin itself. The real wheel opens under your cursor and closes the moment you release.

Sector 01

Actions

Pre-comp, un-precomp, split at the playhead, duplicate, reverse time, save the frame as a PNG, delete.

Sector 02

Transform

Center in the comp, fit to width or height, mirror horizontally or vertically. No numbers typed into the transform group.

Sector 03

Create

Shape, ellipse, polygon, star, solid, null, camera, light, text, adjustment layer — each one made to match the selection’s in and out points.

Sector 04

Blur

Gaussian and Camera Lens on the wheel from the start, with box, directional, radial, fast box, channel and compound a click away in the picker.

Sector 05

Effects

Tint, Fill and Drop Shadow to begin with. The picker reads every effect installed in your copy of After Effects, so any of them can take a slot.

Sector 06

Presets

Empty until you fill it. AEwheel finds your built-in and user .ffx presets, or you browse to one, and it sits on the wheel.

Sector 07

Anchor

No tool list — a nine-point grid. Release on a cell and the anchor snaps there. No Pan-Behind, no nudge-and-undo.

Sector 08

Freeze

Freeze the frame under the playhead, toggle time remapping, hold the last frame to the end of the comp.

04_ Install

Three steps. About two minutes.

The readme in the zip walks through the same three, with screenshots.

01

Run the installer

Close After Effects, run setup.exe from the zip, then open After Effects again. Nothing else to configure.

02

Pin the panel

Open Window › Extensions › AEwheel, dock it wherever suits you, then save your workspace. AEwheel needs its panel open to run, and saving the workspace means it reopens with After Effects every time.

03

Flick

That's it — the wheel is live in your comp. Add your licence key in the panel whenever you're ready; until then you have 7 days of the full plugin, nothing held back.

07_ FAQ

Straight answers.

01 Does it work on Mac?

Not yet — AEwheel is Windows only right now. A Mac build is on the radar and it'll happen if enough of you ask for it. If you're on macOS, email us and you'll be counted.

02 How long is the free trial?

7 days, and it's the complete plugin — every sector, every action, in your real projects. No card and no sign-up to start it. When the 7 days are up the tools lock until you add a licence key; nothing you made with it is touched, and the key turns it straight back on.

03 Which After Effects versions are supported?

After Effects CC 2021 and newer, on Windows 10 and 11. On something older, try the free trial first — it costs nothing to find out.

04 Do I need to keep the panel open?

Yes. AEwheel runs from its panel, so the panel has to be open — docked or floating, either is fine. Dock it once and save your workspace with Window › Workspace › Save as New Workspace and it reopens with After Effects every time.

05 How does the licence key work?

Paste the key into the panel and hit activate. It checks once online, then remembers — after that AEwheel works offline. One key activates on up to 2 machines, so your desktop and laptop are both covered.

06 Is there a refund policy?

No — all purchases are final. That's why the trial is the complete plugin with nothing held back: you get 7 days to use it on real work, and you only pay once you already know it's for you.

07 What if I get a new computer?

Free a slot yourself and move the key across. On the old machine open the panel and hit Deactivate this PC — that releases one of your two activations straight away. Then install AEwheel on the new machine and enter the same key. You can do this as often as you like, so the two activations follow you from machine to machine. Only if the old PC is gone or dead and you couldn't deactivate it first, email support with your order details and we'll reset it for you.

08 Will it slow After Effects down?

No. The wheel is a panel that sits idle until you call it, and it doesn't add effects, expressions or layers of its own to your project. Open a project built with AEwheel on a machine without it and everything still works — the plugin changes how you reach the tools, not what it writes into the comp.
